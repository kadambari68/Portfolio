Sales & Marketing Operations · Build vs. Buy
Email Follow-up Automation — Gmail Add-on
Process Diagram Decision Log Vendor Doc Review
Problem
The team's CRM included a built-in follow-up sequencing feature. It didn't fit how the team actually worked: follow-ups needed to stay anchored to the exact email thread they came from, not a generic contact record. Reps were manually re-finding threads and rebuilding context before every follow-up — the automation meant to save time was adding steps.
My Role
I ran the technical evaluation of the CRM's native feature against the team's actual workflow, documented the gap against vendor product documentation, made the build-vs-buy call, then designed, built, and shipped the replacement — including the sidebar interface reps use daily.
Snapshot
Verified
In production since July 2026. Rolled out to the full marketing team.
Process & Artifact — Before vs. After
Decisions & Trade-offs
DecisionOptions ConsideredChoiceWhy
Where to anchor follow-ups Contact record (CRM default) vs. email thread Email thread Reps think in terms of "this conversation," not "this contact" — matching the mental model cut re-orientation time
Build vs. buy Upgrade CRM tier vs. build in-house Build in-house Confirmed against vendor documentation that thread-level anchoring was a structural limitation, not a tier limitation — upgrading would not have solved it
Distribution Standalone web tool vs. Gmail Add-on Gmail Add-on Reps live in Gmail all day; a separate destination would carry real adoption risk
Maintenance ownership Vendor-supported feature vs. self-maintained script Self-maintained Accepted ongoing maintenance responsibility deliberately, in exchange for an exact fit to the real workflow
Outcome
VerifiedVendor product documentation confirmed thread-anchored follow-ups were not supported by the CRM's native sequencing feature — this finding is what justified the build decision.
VerifiedBuilt and shipped as a Gmail Add-on with a sidebar interface. Rolled out to the marketing team in July 2026 and in active daily use.
EstimatedExpected to meaningfully cut the time reps spend re-locating threads and rebuilding context before each follow-up — not independently timed; a directional estimate, not a measured figure.
Cost avoided: no incremental per-seat licensing for an upgraded CRM tier that wouldn't have solved the actual problem
Adoption risk: minimized by shipping inside the tool reps already use daily, instead of asking them to adopt a new destination
Operational trade-off: took on long-term maintenance ownership in exchange for an exact workflow fit — a deliberate, documented choice

Vendor Selection · CRM Platform
CRM Platform Evaluation & Contract Negotiation
Context
Organisation had no CRM. Sales managed relationships through email and spreadsheets. Goal: select a platform non-technical users would actually adopt, with a viable ERP integration path.
Approach
Weighted evaluation matrix — adoption risk, integration capability, support quality, three-year cost, timeline. Live demos and internal stakeholder sessions for each shortlisted platform.
Outcome
Selected platform on lower adoption risk despite higher list cost. Implementation contract subsequently renegotiated to extend training scope at no added cost.
Security Finding — UAT
During UAT, a demo environment was found surfacing historical production data in active test sessions. Reproduced, documented, and escalated to the vendor's development team — resolved before go-live. This finding was made by the internal reviewer, not flagged by the vendor's own QA.
Build vs. Buy · Pharma Compliance
Electronic Batch Records: Build vs. Buy Analysis
Context
Required an electronic batch manufacturing record system to digitize production documentation — a regulatory requirement in a GMP environment. Specialist vendor quotes evaluated.
Assessment
Specialist vendor solutions come pre-validated but carry high first-year cost and configuration complexity. High fit risk if internal requirements aren't yet stable.
Decision
Internal prototype build initiated to define requirements precisely before any vendor commitment — de-risking a future purchase rather than replacing it outright.
Build vs. Buy Framework
Not a purely cost-driven call. Weighed: validation burden of an internal build in a GMP environment, the risk of configuring a vendor system against unstable requirements, and the strategic value of a prototype in making any future vendor selection more accurate.
